Hagia Sophia vs Blue Mosque: Entry, Differences & Route

Paid gallery vs free mosque visitFlexible same-day routeUpdated 9 September 2026
By Melike · Independent visitor guide · Updated 9 September 2026

Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque offer different visits within the same part of Sultanahmet. The useful decision is what you want to see, what access your visit includes and how much time you have.

Which one should you choose?

Choose Hagia Sophia for the gallery perspective and the building’s Byzantine and Ottoman layers. Choose the Blue Mosque for a free visit focused on Ottoman mosque architecture and tile decoration. If time permits, combine them; neither choice requires buying a bundle.

What the Hagia Sophia tourist route includes

The Ministry’s visitor-management announcement separates tourism from worship and describes a gallery route with views of Ottoman additions and Byzantine mosaics. Do not treat a gallery ticket as unrestricted access to the ground-floor prayer area. Ministry of Culture and Tourism announcement, 12 January 2024.

Read the interior guide to decide which details matter to you. Check the entrance and visitor route before travelling; ticket collection instructions can differ by seller.

What makes the Blue Mosque different

The Blue Mosque offers a close view of Ottoman mosque design, including its Iznik tile decoration, domes and six-minaret exterior. It is an active place of worship, so a visitor’s schedule must fit around its religious use. GoTürkiye’s Blue Mosque guide.

Ordinary mosque visits are free. The mosque’s visitor guide lists 08:30–19:00 from April through September and 08:30–17:00 from October through March, with last visitor entry 30 minutes before closing. On Fridays, visitor access starts at 14:30. At noon and afternoon prayers, it lists closure from 30 minutes before the call to prayer until 15 minutes after prayer finishes. Blue Mosque visitor rules, checked 9 September 2026.

A same-day route with room for prayer closures

  1. Check the day’s access first. Look at Blue Mosque visitor notices and your Hagia Sophia booking instructions. Use these as the fixed points; do not reserve consecutive slots merely because the buildings are close.
  2. Choose the first stop. If the Blue Mosque is open when you arrive and your Hagia Sophia booking is flexible, a free mosque visit can come first. If the mosque is closed to visitors, plan Hagia Sophia or the square first, subject to its own access rules.
  3. Keep a transition buffer. Allow about 15–20 minutes to cross the square, orient yourself and reach the correct visitor entrance. This is a planning buffer, not a walking-time measurement.
  4. Recheck before entering the second site. If access is paused, wait for the permitted visiting period. Avoid planning an immediate third booking after the second stop.

For a first draft of the day, reserve around three hours for the pair, then increase that allowance for queues or closures. On a Friday, place the Blue Mosque after its published visitor opening time. Our Sultanahmet day itinerary gives a wider framework.

Dress, worship and practical access

Check the dress-code guide before leaving your hotel and follow the signs at each entrance. Leave time to prepare for entry rather than doing so at the front of a queue.

If step-free access, a wheelchair, a stroller or a place to rest is essential, confirm the actual visitor route with each venue. The proximity of the two landmarks does not mean their internal access arrangements are the same.

When a paid tour adds something

A tour may be useful if you want a guide to interpret both buildings and manage the route. Compare the guide’s language, meeting point, group arrangements and which admission is included. A Blue Mosque audio guide or walking tour is an optional service; it is not a mandatory mosque-entry fee.

You can visit independently using the Hagia Sophia admission guide. If you prefer a packaged route, compare ticket and tour options against buying only the services you need.

Questions before you book

Are Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque the same building?

No. They are separate landmarks facing the Sultanahmet area. Hagia Sophia has a paid tourist-gallery route; the Blue Mosque offers free visitor access during its permitted visiting periods.

Do I need a ticket to enter the Blue Mosque?

The mosque’s visitor guide says visits are free. A paid tour can charge for guiding or other services; it does not turn ordinary mosque admission into a paid ticket.

Can I visit both in one day?

Yes. For planning, allow around three hours including a modest buffer, then add extra time for queues or prayer closures. Choose the order around actual access and any timed booking.

Which should I visit first on a Friday?

The Blue Mosque’s published visitor start time on Fridays is 14:30. Plan it later and arrange Hagia Sophia separately around its current visitor access and your ticket conditions.
